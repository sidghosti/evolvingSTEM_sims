Bead Experiment Simulation

A simulated version of the EvolvingSTEM bead experiment. Grow a culture of bacteria and watch as they divide (binary fission). Every now and then, a mutant will arise (check "Show mutants" to see them as different colors!). These mutants can then become more or less common through selection (variants that survive and reproduce better in this environment increase) or drift (random ups and downs in frequency, strongest in small populations and at each transfer). Add beads to provide a surface for biofilm-forming mutants to attach to and observe how this selective force causes the mutant frequencies change over multiple passages. And most important... have fun!

🔍 What's simplified in this simulation?
🧫Tiny population
In the sim: About 1,000 cells.
In real life: Billions in one tube.
Why: So you can watch every cell.
💡 A real tube holds more bacteria than there are people on Earth.
🎲Lots of mutations
In the sim: About 1 in 17 divisions.
In real life: About 1 in a few hundred.
Why: With only 1,000 cells, real rates would give almost no mutants to see.
💡 A real tube has so many divisions that every DNA letter gets mutated in some cell.
🎨Colorful mutants
In the sim: “Show mutants” colors each mutant.
In real life: Mutant cells look the same.
Why: To show hidden DNA changes that scientists find only by sequencing.
💡 bmo was invisible on plates, so it was found only by sequencing.
🧬Three biofilm genes
In the sim: Only bmo, fuzY and wspF help on beads.
In real life: Other genes (wspA, yfiR, morA) can too.
Why: These are real mutations found by EvolvingSTEM students.
💡 wspF caused 33 of 57 sequenced wrinkly colonies.

⚙️ Advanced settings

Per-lineage parameters, mostly for instructor use. Defaults are tuned so the simulation shows the same overall pattern seen in real EvolvingSTEM bead populations, not measured values. Biofilm-forming capacity: each tick a cell touches a surface (the dish wall or a bead), it has that % chance of sticking there. Other bacteria don't count as a surface yet, so a biofilm can only form as a single layer along the wall.
